"Constrained optimization with inequalities $g(x) \le 0$" KKT Conditions Stationarity ($\nabla f + \sum \mu_i \nabla g_i = 0$), Primal feasibility ($g_i \le 0$), Dual feasibility ($\mu_i \ge 0$), Complementary Slackness ($\mu_i g_i = 0$).

PART 1: LINEAR ALGEBRA GATE CS (Sec 1) & DA (Sec 2)

1.1 Matrices, Determinants, Inverses & Adjoints

Determinant Properties & Special Matrix Identities

Let $A, B$ be $n \times n$ matrices and $c$ be a scalar:

$$\det(cA) = c^n \det(A), \quad \det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B), \quad \det(A^{-1}) = \frac{1}{\det(A)}$$ $$\det(\text{adj}(A)) = (\det(A))^{n-1}, \quad \text{adj}(\text{adj}(A)) = (\det(A))^{n-2} A$$ $$\det(\text{adj}(\text{adj}(A))) = (\det(A))^{(n-1)^2}$$
WHEN TO USE: Whenever GATE asks for the determinant of a scaled matrix, adjoint matrix, or compound expression like $|2 \text{adj}(3A)|$.
  • Step 1: Pull out constants using $c^n$.
  • Step 2: Replace $\det(\text{adj}(M))$ with $|M|^{n-1}$.
  • Step 3: Combine powers of $|A|$.
GATE TRAP: Students consistently write $\det(cA) = c \det(A)$. The scalar $c$ factors out of every row, yielding $c^n$! Also, for skew-symmetric matrices $A^T = -A$: if $n$ is odd, $\det(A) = 0$ always. If $n$ is even, $\det(A)$ is a perfect square.

1.2 Vector Spaces, Subspaces, Basis & Dimension

Subspace Criteria & Dimension Rules

A subset $W \subseteq V$ is a subspace if and only if:

$$\mathbf{0} \in W \quad \text{and} \quad \forall u, v \in W, \; \alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{R} \implies \alpha u + \beta v \in W$$

For two subspaces $W_1, W_2 \subseteq V$:

$$\dim(W_1 + W_2) = \dim(W_1) + \dim(W_2) - \dim(W_1 \cap W_2)$$
WHEN TO USE:
  • To check if a set is a subspace: check if setting all variables to $0$ satisfies the equation. If $0 \notin W$, immediately reject. Also, reject any equation containing affine constants ($x_1 + x_2 = 1$), products ($x_1 x_2 = 0$), or inequalities ($x_1 \ge 0$).
  • To find dimension of symmetric matrices: $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.
  • To find dimension of skew-symmetric matrices: $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$.
  • To find dimension of trace-zero matrices: $n^2 - 1$.

1.3 Rank, Nullity & The 4 Fundamental Subspaces

Rank-Nullity Theorem & Orthogonal Complements

For any matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ ($m$ rows, $n$ columns):

$$\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Nullity}(A) = n \quad (\text{number of columns})$$ $$\text{Col}(A) \perp \text{Null}(A^T) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{R}^m, \qquad \text{Row}(A) \perp \text{Null}(A) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{R}^n$$ $$\dim(\text{Col}(A)) = \dim(\text{Row}(A)) = \text{Rank}(A) = r$$ $$\dim(\text{Null}(A)) = n - r, \qquad \dim(\text{Null}(A^T)) = m - r$$
WHEN TO USE: Whenever asked for "dimension of solution space of $Ax=0$" (this is simply $\text{Nullity}(A) = n - r$), or when asked whether a vector is orthogonal to the row space (check if $Ax = 0$).
OUTER PRODUCT SHORTCUT: If $u \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times 1}$ and $v \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1}$ are non-zero vectors, the matrix $A = u v^T$ ALWAYS has $\text{Rank}(A) = 1$. Its non-zero eigenvalue is $\lambda = v^T u = \text{Tr}(A)$, and all other $n-1$ eigenvalues are $0$.

1.4 Systems of Linear Equations ($Ax = b$)

Rouché-Capelli Theorem & Consistency Classification
Condition System Type Solution Nature Degrees of Freedom
$\text{Rank}(A) \neq \text{Rank}([A|b])$ Inconsistent No Solution None
$\text{Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}([A|b]) = n$ Consistent Unique Solution $0$ free variables
$\text{Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}([A|b]) = r < n$ Consistent Infinitely Many Solutions $k = n - r$ free variables
WHEN TO USE: Parametric questions with unknowns $\lambda, \mu$ in the matrix:
  • Step 1: Convert $[A|b]$ to Upper Triangular / Row Echelon Form using elementary row operations.
  • Step 2: Inspect the bottom row $[0 \quad 0 \quad (\alpha) \;|\; (\beta)]$.
    • No solution: $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta \neq 0$.
    • Infinitely many: $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 0$.
    • Unique solution: $\alpha \neq 0$ (independent of $\beta$).

1.5 Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors, Cayley-Hamilton & Diagonalization

Eigenvalue Theorems & Invariants

For characteristic equation $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \text{Trace}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}, \qquad \prod_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \det(A)$$ $$A \mathbf{v} = \lambda \mathbf{v} \implies A^k \mathbf{v} = \lambda^k \mathbf{v}, \quad (A + cI)\mathbf{v} = (\lambda + c)\mathbf{v}, \quad A^{-1}\mathbf{v} = \frac{1}{\lambda}\mathbf{v}$$
10-SECOND INSPECTION TRICK: If every row of matrix $A$ sums to a constant $S$, then $\lambda = S$ is GUARANTEED to be an eigenvalue of $A$, with corresponding eigenvector $\mathbf{v} = [1, 1, \dots, 1]^T$.
Cayley-Hamilton Theorem & Diagonalizability

Every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation: $p(A) = O$.

$$A \text{ is diagonalizable} \iff \text{Geometric Multiplicity}(\lambda_i) = \text{Algebraic Multiplicity}(\lambda_i) \quad \forall i$$ $$\text{GM}(\lambda) = \text{Nullity}(A - \lambda I) = n - \text{Rank}(A - \lambda I)$$
GATE TRAP: Having distinct eigenvalues is a sufficient condition for diagonalizability, NOT a necessary condition! The identity matrix $I$ has repeated eigenvalue $\lambda = 1$ with $\text{AM}=n, \text{GM}=n$, and is already diagonal.

1.6 Matrix Factorizations & Decompositions (LU, Cholesky, QR, SVD)

Decomposition Summary Table
Decomposition Form & Requirements Properties of Factors Primary Application / Exam Trigger
LU Decomposition $A = LU$
Requires all leading principal minors $\neq 0$.		 $L$: Unit lower triangular ($l_{ii}=1$).
$U$: Upper triangular.		 Solving $Ax=b$ via forward substitution ($Ly=b$) then back substitution ($Ux=y$). $\det(A)=\prod u_{ii}$.
Cholesky $A = L L^T$
Requires $A$ to be Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD).		 $L$: Lower triangular with positive diagonal elements $l_{ii} > 0$. Half the FLOPs of LU. Check SPD via Sylvester's Criterion: all leading principal minors $> 0$.
QR Decomposition $A = QR$
$A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ with full column rank.		 $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$: Orthogonal columns ($Q^T Q = I$).
$R \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$: Upper triangular, invertible.		 Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization, Least Squares Solution: $\hat{x} = R^{-1} Q^T b$.
Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) $A = U \Sigma V^T$
Valid for ANY $m \times n$ matrix!		 $U \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ (eigenvectors of $AA^T$).
$V \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ (eigenvectors of $A^T A$).
$\Sigma$: diagonal singular values $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \dots \ge 0$.		 $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i(A^T A)}$. Rank = count of $\sigma_i > 0$. Low-rank approximation (Eckart-Young Theorem). Spectral norm $\|A\|_2 = \sigma_1$.

PART 2: PROBABILITY & STATISTICS GATE CS (Sec 1) & DA (Sec 1)

2.1 Axioms, Conditional Probability & Bayes' Theorem

Foundational Rules of Probability
$$P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$$ $$P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \quad (P(B) > 0)$$ $$P(A \cap B) = P(A \mid B) P(B) = P(B \mid A) P(A)$$

Total Probability Theorem & Bayes' Theorem: For partition $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_k$ of sample space:

$$P(A) = \sum_{i=1}^k P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)$$ $$P(B_j \mid A) = \frac{P(A \mid B_j) P(B_j)}{\sum_{i=1}^k P(A \mid B_i) P(B_i)}$$
WHEN TO USE: Any problem stating "A test is 95% accurate. Disease incidence is 1%. Find probability of disease given a positive test."
  • $P(D)$ is the prior probability.
  • $P(+ \mid D)$ is the sensitivity.
  • $P(+ \mid D^c)$ is the false positive rate ($1 - \text{specificity}$).
  • Denom: $P(+) = P(+ \mid D)P(D) + P(+ \mid D^c)P(D^c)$.
GATE TRAP ON INDEPENDENCE: $A$ and $B$ are independent $\iff P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$. Mutually exclusive events with $P(A)>0, P(B)>0$ are NEVER independent because $P(A \cap B) = 0 \neq P(A)P(B)$! Pairwise independence does NOT imply mutual independence.

2.2 Discrete Random Variables & Standard Distributions

Discrete Distributions Cheat Sheet
Distribution Probability Mass Function (PMF) $P(X=k)$ Mean $E[X]$ Variance $\text{Var}(X)$ When to Apply in GATE
Bernoulli $(p)$ $p^k (1-p)^{1-k}, \quad k \in \{0, 1\}$ $p$ $p(1-p)$ Single binary trial (Coin flip, packet drop).
Binomial $(n, p)$ $\binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}, \quad k \in \{0, \dots, n\}$ $np$ $np(1-p)$ Number of successes in $n$ independent trials with replacement.
Poisson $(\lambda)$ $\frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}, \quad k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ $\lambda$ $\lambda$ (Mean = Var!) Rare events in continuous time/space; limit of Binomial as $n \to \infty, p \to 0, np = \lambda$.
Geometric $(p)$ $(1-p)^{k-1} p, \quad k = 1, 2, \dots$ (trial of 1st success) $\frac{1}{p}$ $\frac{1-p}{p^2}$ Number of trials until 1st success. Memoryless: $P(X > s+t \mid X > s) = P(X > t) = (1-p)^t$.
Hypergeometric $\frac{\binom{K}{k}\binom{N-K}{n-k}}{\binom{N}{n}}$ $n \frac{K}{N}$ $n \frac{K}{N} \left(1-\frac{K}{N}\right) \frac{N-n}{N-1}$ Sampling without replacement from a finite population of size $N$ with $K$ successes.

Continuous Distributions Master Summary

Uniform $U(a, b)$ $f(x) = \frac{1}{b-a}, \quad x \in [a, b]$ $\frac{x-a}{b-a}$ $E[X] = \frac{a+b}{2}$
$\text{Var}(X) = \frac{(b-a)^2}{12}$		 Max entropy distribution on a bounded interval. Probability is simply length ratio: $\frac{d-c}{b-a}$.

$\text{Var} = \frac{k}{k-2} \; (k>2)$		 Heavier tails than Normal. Approaches $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ as $k \to \infty$. Used when population $\sigma$ is unknown.

2.4 Joint Distributions, Covariance & Expectation Algebra

Joint Distributions & Covariance Formulas
$$\text{Marginal: } f_X(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f_{X,Y}(x, y) \, dy, \qquad f_{Y|X}(y \mid x) = \frac{f_{X,Y}(x, y)}{f_X(x)}$$ $$\text{Independence: } f_{X,Y}(x, y) = f_X(x) f_Y(y) \iff X \perp Y$$ $$\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y], \qquad \rho_{X,Y} = \frac{\text{Cov}(X, Y)}{\sigma_X \sigma_Y} \in [-1, 1]$$ $$\text{Var}(aX + bY) = a^2 \text{Var}(X) + b^2 \text{Var}(Y) + 2ab \, \text{Cov}(X, Y)$$
GATE TRAP ON CORRELATION: Zero correlation ($\text{Cov}(X,Y) = 0$) does NOT imply independence! Counterexample: Let $X \sim U(-1, 1)$ and $Y = X^2$. Then $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[X^3] - E[X]E[X^2] = 0 - 0 = 0$, but $Y$ is completely determined by $X$! (Note: Zero correlation DOES imply independence ONLY IF $X, Y$ are jointly Gaussian).

2.5 Probability Inequalities & Limit Theorems

Markov, Chebyshev, WLLN & CLT
Theorem / Inequality Prerequisites Mathematical Statement When to Use in GATE
Markov's Inequality $X \ge 0$ (non-negative RV) and $a > 0$. $P(X \ge a) \le \frac{E[X]}{a}$ When ONLY the mean $E[X]$ is known, and $X$ cannot be negative.
Chebyshev's Inequality Any RV with finite mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. $P(|X - \mu| \ge k\sigma) \le \frac{1}{k^2}$
$P(|X - \mu| \ge \epsilon) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{\epsilon^2}$		 When both mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$ are known, but distribution shape is completely unknown.
Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN) $X_1, \dots, X_n$ i.i.d. with mean $\mu$ and finite variance. $\lim_{n \to \infty} P(|\bar{X}_n - \mu| \ge \epsilon) = 0$ Convergence in probability of sample mean to population mean.
Central Limit Theorem (CLT) $X_1, \dots, X_n$ i.i.d. with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. $Z_n = \frac{\sum X_i - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} = \frac{\bar{X}_n - \mu}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ To approximate the probability of a sum or average of $n \ge 30$ independent random variables.

2.6 Mathematical Statistics, Estimation & Hypothesis Testing (DA Sec 1)

Estimators & Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)

Sample Statistics:

$$\text{Sample Mean: } \bar{X} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i, \quad E[\bar{X}] = \mu, \quad \text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}$$ $$\text{Unbiased Sample Variance: } S^2 = \frac{1}{n-1} \sum_{i=1}^n (X_i - \bar{X})^2, \quad E[S^2] = \sigma^2$$

Maximum Likelihood Estimation Recipe:

$$L(\theta) = \prod_{i=1}^n f(x_i; \theta) \implies \ln L(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \ln f(x_i; \theta)$$ $$\text{Set } \frac{\partial \ln L(\theta)}{\partial \theta} = 0 \quad \text{and check } \frac{\partial^2 \ln L}{\partial \theta^2} < 0$$
STANDARD MLE SHORTCUTS (Memorize for GATE):
  • Bernoulli$(p)$: $\hat{p}_{\text{MLE}} = \bar{X}$ (sample proportion).
  • Poisson$(\lambda)$: $\hat{\lambda}_{\text{MLE}} = \bar{X}$.
  • Exponential$(\lambda)$: $\hat{\lambda}_{\text{MLE}} = \frac{1}{\bar{X}}$.
  • Normal$(\mu, \sigma^2)$: $\hat{\mu}_{\text{MLE}} = \bar{X}$, but $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{MLE}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum(X_i-\bar{X})^2$ (biased! Divisor is $n$, NOT $n-1$).
  • Uniform$[0, \theta]$: $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}} = X_{(n)} = \max(X_1, \dots, X_n)$ (found via boundary check, NOT derivative!).

WHEN TO USE $Z$-TEST VS $t$-TEST:
  • Use $Z$-Test ($Z = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{\sigma / \sqrt{n}}$): When population variance $\sigma^2$ is KNOWN, or sample size $n \ge 30$ (by CLT).
  • Use $t$-Test ($t = \frac{\bar{X} - \mu_0}{S / \sqrt{n}}$ with $\nu = n-1$ degrees of freedom): When population is Normal, $\sigma^2$ is UNKNOWN, and sample size is small ($n < 30$).

PART 3: DISCRETE MATHEMATICS GATE CS (Sec 1 Core) & DA (Graphs/AI)

3.1 Propositional & First-Order Predicate Logic

Core Logical Equivalences & Implications
$$p \to q \equiv \neg p \vee q \equiv \neg q \to \neg p \quad (\text{Contrapositive})$$ $$\neg(p \to q) \equiv p \wedge \neg q \quad (\text{Negation of implication})$$ $$p \leftrightarrow q \equiv (p \to q) \wedge (q \to p) \equiv (p \wedge q) \vee (\neg p \wedge \neg q)$$ $$\text{De Morgan: } \neg(p \wedge q) \equiv \neg p \vee \neg q, \qquad \neg(p \vee q) \equiv \neg p \wedge \neg q$$
WHEN TO USE:
  • To prove a statement is a Tautology: Show its truth value is ALWAYS True (or negation is unsatisfiable).
  • To invalidate an implication $A \to B$: Find a truth assignment where Premise $A = \text{True}$ and Conclusion $B = \text{False}$.
QUANTIFIER DISTRIBUTION TRAPS:
  • $\forall x (P(x) \wedge Q(x)) \equiv (\forall x P(x)) \wedge (\forall x Q(x))$  [VALID]
  • $\forall x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \not\equiv (\forall x P(x)) \vee (\forall x Q(x))$  [INVALID TRAP!] (LHS can be true while neither is universally true, e.g., "every integer is even or odd").
  • $\exists x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \equiv (\exists x P(x)) \vee (\exists x Q(x))$  [VALID]
  • $\exists x (P(x) \wedge Q(x)) \not\equiv (\exists x P(x)) \wedge (\exists x Q(x))$  [INVALID TRAP!]

3.2 Sets, Relations, POSETs & Lattices

Relations Counting on a Set of Size $n$
Relation Property Formal Condition on $R \subseteq A \times A$ Exact Formula ($|A| = n$)
Total number of relations Any subset of $A \times A$ $2^{n^2}$
Reflexive $\forall a \in A, (a, a) \in R$ $2^{n(n-1)} = 2^{n^2 - n}$
Irreflexive $\forall a \in A, (a, a) \notin R$ $2^{n(n-1)}$
Symmetric $(a, b) \in R \implies (b, a) \in R$ $2^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}$
Reflexive & Symmetric Diagonal fixed present, upper triangle choices $2^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}$

Asymmetric $(a, b) \in R \implies (b, a) \notin R$ (no diagonals) $3^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}$
POSETs & Lattices

A relation is a POSET if it is Reflexive, Antisymmetric, and Transitive.

A POSET is a Lattice if for EVERY pair of elements $a, b$, there exists a unique Least Upper Bound ($a \vee b$, Join) and unique Greatest Lower Bound ($a \wedge b$, Meet).

LATTICE INSPECTION RULE: In a Hasse diagram, if two elements have multiple minimal upper bounds (i.e., paths diverge and meet at two incomparable vertices without a common lower one), it is NOT a lattice!

3.4 Graph Theory

Handshaking, Planarity, Trees & Coloring Formulas
$$\text{Handshaking Lemma: } \sum_{v \in V} \deg(v) = 2|E| \implies \text{Count of odd-degree vertices is always EVEN}$$ $$\text{Tree with } n \text{ vertices has exactly } n-1 \text{ edges. Cayley's Formula: } n^{n-2} \text{ labelled trees}$$ $$\text{Euler's Planar Formula: } V - E + R = 1 + C \quad (\text{For connected: } V - E + R = 2)$$ $$\text{Planar Edge Bounds: } E \le 3V - 6 \quad (\text{If triangle-free / bipartite: } E \le 2V - 4)$$
WHEN TO USE EULERIAN VS HAMILTONIAN CRITERIA:
  • Eulerian Circuit: Connected graph where EVERY vertex has an EVEN degree.
  • Eulerian Path (Trail): Connected graph with EXACTLY TWO vertices of odd degree (start and end).
  • Hamiltonian Cycle: Traverses every vertex exactly once. (NP-complete in general. Sufficient condition: Dirac's theorem: if $n \ge 3$ and $\deg(v) \ge n/2$ for all $v$, graph is Hamiltonian).
STANDARD CHROMATIC NUMBERS $\chi(G)$ (10-Second Recall):
  • Complete graph $K_n$: $\chi(K_n) = n$.
  • Bipartite graph $K_{m,n}$ ($E > 0$): $\chi(G) = 2$ (Graph is 2-colorable $\iff$ bipartite $\iff$ no odd cycles).
  • Odd cycle $C_{2k+1}$: $\chi(C_{2k+1}) = 3$. Even cycle $C_{2k}$: $\chi(C_{2k}) = 2$.
  • Tree ($n \ge 2$): $\chi(T) = 2$.
  • Planar graph (Four Color Theorem): $\chi(G) \le 4$.

PART 4: CALCULUS & OPTIMIZATION GATE CS (Sec 1) & DA (Sec 3)

4.1 Limits, Continuity & Mean Value Theorems

L'Hôpital's Rule & Mean Value Theorems

L'Hôpital's Rule: If $\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ evaluates to $\left[\frac{0}{0}\right]$ or $\left[\frac{\pm\infty}{\pm\infty}\right]$:

$$\lim_{x \to c} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to c} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$
Theorem Hypothesis on $[a, b]$ Guaranteed Conclusion
Rolle's Theorem Continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, and $f(a) = f(b)$. $\exists c \in (a, b)$ such that $f'(c) = 0$.
Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem (LMVT) Continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$. $\exists c \in (a, b)$ such that $f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$.
Cauchy's MVT $f, g$ continuous on $[a,b]$, differentiable on $(a,b)$, $g'(x) \neq 0$. $\exists c \in (a, b)$ such that $\frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{g(b) - g(a)}$.
STANDARD LIMIT SHORTCUTS: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2}, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x - 1}{x} = 1, \quad \lim_{x \to 0} (1 + x)^{1/x} = e$$

4.2 Single-Variable Maxima, Minima & Leibniz Integral Rule

Derivative Tests & Leibniz Differentiation Under Integral

Critical Points: Points where $f'(x) = 0$ or $f'(x)$ does not exist.

$$f''(x_0) > 0 \implies \text{Local Minimum at } x_0$$ $$f''(x_0) < 0 \implies \text{Local Maximum at } x_0$$ $$f''(x_0) = 0 \implies \text{Inconclusive (test higher derivatives: if 1st non-vanishing is odd order } \implies \text{inflection point)}$$

Leibniz Rule: For differentiating an integral with variable limits:

$$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ \int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f(t) \, dt \right] = f(v(x)) \cdot v'(x) - f(u(x)) \cdot u'(x)$$
WHEN TO USE LEIBNIZ: Whenever GATE asks for $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\int_0^{x^2} \sin(t) dt}{x^4}$. Apply L'Hôpital by differentiating numerator using Leibniz rule!

4.3 Multivariable Calculus: Gradients, Directional Derivatives & Hessians

Multivariable Differential Operators
$$\text{Gradient: } \nabla f(\mathbf{x}) = \left[ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2}, \dots, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n} \right]^T$$ $$\text{Directional Derivative in direction of unit vector } \mathbf{u}: \quad D_{\mathbf{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u} = \|\nabla f\| \cos \theta$$ $$\text{Hessian Matrix: } H(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1^2} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_2} \\ \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2 \partial x_1} & \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_2^2} \end{bmatrix}$$
KEY DIRECTIONAL DERIVATIVE FACTS:
  • Maximum rate of increase of $f$ occurs in the direction of the gradient $\nabla f$, and the maximum rate equals $\|\nabla f\|$.
  • Maximum rate of decrease occurs in the direction of $-\nabla f$, with rate $-\|\nabla f\|$.
  • Rate of change is zero in any direction orthogonal to $\nabla f$ (tangent to the level curve/surface).

4.4 Multivariable Optimization (Unconstrained & Constrained)

Stationary Point Classification in 2 Variables

Set $\nabla f(x, y) = \mathbf{0} \implies \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 0, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 0$. Let $r = f_{xx}, s = f_{xy}, t = f_{yy}$ and discriminant $D = rt - s^2 = \det(H)$:

Discriminant $D = rt - s^2$ $r = f_{xx}$ Hessian Eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ Classification of $(x_0, y_0)$
$D > 0$ $r > 0$ $\lambda_1 > 0, \lambda_2 > 0$ (Positive Definite) Local Minimum
$D > 0$ $r < 0$ $\lambda_1 < 0, \lambda_2 < 0$ (Negative Definite) Local Maximum
$D < 0$ Any $\lambda_1 \cdot \lambda_2 < 0$ (Indefinite: opposite signs) Saddle Point
$D = 0$ Any At least one eigenvalue is $0$ Inconclusive (requires higher order analysis)

4.5 Convexity, KKT Conditions & Gradient Descent (DA Sec 3)

Convex Functions & Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Conditions

Convexity Criteria:

$$\text{Def: } f(\theta x + (1-\theta)y) \le \theta f(x) + (1-\theta)f(y) \quad \forall \theta \in [0, 1]$$ $$\text{1st-Order: } f(y) \ge f(x) + \nabla f(x)^T (y - x)$$ $$\text{2nd-Order: } \nabla^2 f(x) = H(x) \succeq 0 \quad (\text{Hessian is Positive Semi-Definite everywhere})$$
CONVEXITY SUPER-PROPERTY: For any convex function over a convex set, ANY local minimum is GUARANTEED to be a global minimum! If $f$ is strictly convex ($H \succ 0$), the global minimum is unique.
KKT Conditions for Non-Linear Optimization

To minimize $f(x)$ subject to equality constraints $h_j(x) = 0$ ($j=1..m$) and inequality constraints $g_i(x) \le 0$ ($i=1..k$):

$$\mathcal{L}(x, \lambda, \mu) = f(x) + \sum_{j=1}^m \lambda_j h_j(x) + \sum_{i=1}^k \mu_i g_i(x)$$ $$\text{1. Stationarity: } \nabla_x \mathcal{L} = \nabla f(x^*) + \sum_{j=1}^m \lambda_j \nabla h_j(x^*) + \sum_{i=1}^k \mu_i \nabla g_i(x^*) = \mathbf{0}$$ $$\text{2. Primal Feasibility: } g_i(x^*) \le 0 \quad (\forall i), \quad h_j(x^*) = 0 \quad (\forall j)$$ $$\text{3. Dual Feasibility: } \mu_i \ge 0 \quad (\forall i)$$ $$\text{4. Complementary Slackness: } \mu_i g_i(x^*) = 0 \quad (\forall i)$$
HOW TO SOLVE KKT PROBLEMS IN GATE:
  • Examine complementary slackness $\mu_i g_i(x) = 0$. For each inequality, branch into two cases:
    • Case A (Inactive constraint): $g_i(x) < 0 \implies \mu_i = 0$. Solve unconstrained gradient equation.
    • Case B (Active constraint): $g_i(x) = 0 \implies \mu_i \ge 0$. Solve boundary system.
Gradient Descent Algorithm
$$x^{(t+1)} = x^{(t)} - \eta \nabla f(x^{(t)})$$

Where $\eta > 0$ is the learning rate. For $L$-Lipschitz smooth functions ($\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\| \le L \|x - y\|$), convergence is guaranteed when $\eta < \frac{2}{L}$.

PART 5: MASTER 40 GATE TRAPS DIRECTORY High-Risk Pitfalls Across All 4 Modules

Review this checklist the evening before your exam. These 40 traps account for over 80% of negative marks in GATE Engineering Mathematics:

# Topic Common Student Misconception Mathematical Fact / Correct Rule
1 Linear Algebra Assuming $\det(A+B) = \det(A) + \det(B)$. FALSE! In general, $\det(A+B) \neq \det(A) + \det(B)$. Determinant is NOT linear.
2 Linear Algebra Writing $\det(cA) = c\det(A)$ for $n \times n$ matrix. $\det(cA) = c^n \det(A)$. Factor $c$ from each of the $n$ rows.
3 Linear Algebra Assuming $AB = 0 \implies A = 0$ or $B = 0$. Matrices have non-zero zero divisors! Example: $\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}^2 = 0$.
4 Linear Algebra Believing repeated eigenvalues mean non-diagonalizable. Diagonalizability requires $\text{GM} = \text{AM}$. The identity matrix $I$ has repeated $\lambda$ and is diagonal!
5 Linear Algebra Assuming orthogonal matrix must have symmetric entries. Orthogonal means $Q^T Q = I$. Its columns are orthonormal. Eigenvalues are on unit circle ($|\lambda|=1$).
6 Linear Algebra Assuming rank of product equals product of ranks. $\text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$. By Sylvester: $\text{Rank}(AB) \ge \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n$.
7 Prob & Stats Assuming Mutually Exclusive means Independent. For positive probability events, mutually exclusive events can NEVER be independent ($P(A \cap B) = 0 \neq P(A)P(B)$).
8 Prob & Stats Assuming $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0 \implies X, Y$ are independent. Zero covariance only measures lack of linear association. Non-linear dependence can still exist (e.g. $Y = X^2$).
9 Prob & Stats Dividing by $n$ when computing unbiased sample variance $S^2$. Unbiased sample variance uses divisor $(n-1)$: $S^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum(X_i-\bar{X})^2$. MLE uses divisor $n$.
10 Prob & Stats Applying Chebyshev to non-symmetric distributions incorrectly. $P(|X-\mu|\ge k\sigma) \le \frac{1}{k^2}$ holds for any distribution with finite variance. But one-sided bounds cannot simply be halved!
11 Prob & Stats Confusing Type I error and Type II error. $\alpha = P(\text{Reject } H_0 \mid H_0 \text{ is True})$. $\beta = P(\text{Fail to reject } H_0 \mid H_0 \text{ is False})$. Power = $1 - \beta$.
12 Discrete Math Assuming $\forall x (P(x) \vee Q(x)) \equiv \forall x P(x) \vee \forall x Q(x)$. INVALID! Universal quantifier $\forall$ does NOT distribute over disjunction $\vee$.
13 Discrete Math Believing Empty Relation is Reflexive. On a non-empty set $A$, empty relation $\emptyset$ is Irreflexive, Symmetric, and Transitive, but NOT reflexive!
14 Discrete Math Assuming every POSET is a lattice. A POSET is a lattice only if EVERY pair of elements has a UNIQUE LUB and UNIQUE GLB.
15 Discrete Math Counting graphs: confusing labelled vs unlabelled. Number of labelled graphs on $n$ vertices is $2^{\binom{n}{2}}$. Number of labelled trees is $n^{n-2}$.
16 Discrete Math Assuming planar graph cannot have triangles. Planar graphs can have triangles! $E \le 3V - 6$ is the bound. If triangle-free, the tighter bound $E \le 2V - 4$ applies.
17 Calculus Applying L'Hôpital's rule without checking $\left[\frac{0}{0}\right]$ or $\left[\frac{\infty}{\infty}\right]$. If $\lim \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ is finite/finite, differentiating gives a completely WRONG answer. Always check indeterminate form first.
18 Calculus Assuming $f'(c) = 0 \implies$ local extremum. $f'(c) = 0$ is only a necessary condition. $f(x) = x^3$ has $f'(0) = 0$, but $x=0$ is an inflection point, not an extremum!
19 Calculus Forgetting boundary endpoints in closed interval optimization. Global max/min on $[a, b]$ can occur at critical points OR at the boundary endpoints $a$ or $b$. Always evaluate $f(a)$ and $f(b)$!
20 Calculus & Opt In 2D optimization: claiming $D = rt - s^2 < 0$ means inconclusive. If $D < 0$, it is definitively a SADDLE POINT! It is inconclusive ONLY if $D = 0$.
21 Optimization Assuming KKT multiplier for inequality can be negative. For minimization with $g_i(x) \le 0$, the dual feasibility condition mandates $\mu_i \ge 0$. If you get $\mu_i < 0$, that constraint is inactive!
